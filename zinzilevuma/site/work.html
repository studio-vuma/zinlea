Concept studies

These are concept studies, not client projects. The brands are imagined.

I wrote them to show how I approach a brand’s story, provenance and positioning, so you can see how I think before we speak.

Concept study

A coffee estate in the Eastern Highlands of Zimbabwe

The brand

An imagined family estate growing speciality coffee near Chipinge. Most of its harvest goes to roasters abroad, where the estate’s name disappears into someone else’s blend.

The question

How does a grower become a name people ask for, rather than an origin printed in small type on someone else’s bag?

How I would approach it

I would start by defining what the estate’s provenance actually means: the altitude, the family, the way the harvest is handled, and what the coffee tastes like because of them. From that comes one specific thing to be known for.

The identity and packaging would carry it plainly. Scarcity would be real rather than staged: a small, named lot each year, offered first to the people who already know the estate, and priced to reflect how little of it there is.

Concept study

A leather workshop in Nairobi

The brand

An imagined workshop of a few artisans making bags and small leather goods by hand, sold through its own shop and a handful of stockists.

The question

Stockists keep asking for lower prices and more colours. How does the workshop hold its value and grow without becoming ordinary?

How I would approach it

I would write the workshop’s story around its makers and their methods, so a customer understands what the hours of hand work mean. Positioning would set out clearly who the bags are for, and who they are not for.

A small core range would stay available, with limited pieces made in numbers the workshop can honour. Pricing would follow the story, so the value is clear in the shop before anyone has to ask.

Concept study

A safari lodge in the Okavango Delta

The brand

An imagined owner-run lodge with a handful of tents, competing for the attention of travellers who are also looking at much larger names.

The question

Every lodge promises wildlife and quiet. What can this one become known for?

How I would approach it

I would look for the thing guests remember months later and that no one else can claim, perhaps the guides and their knowledge of one stretch of water. That becomes the lodge’s provenance, defined in a sentence and shown everywhere: the website, the welcome, the way a guide introduces the morning.

Ease would be the measure of every detail, from the first enquiry to the drive back to the airstrip.
