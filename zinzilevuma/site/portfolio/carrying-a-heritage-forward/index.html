Portfolio  /  Case 01

Advisory portfolio · Case 01 · Academic placement

Carrying a heritage forward, online.

A curated platform for independent British designers had built its following in pop-up rooms, one conversation at a time. The question was how to carry that heritage online without losing the craft and storytelling that set it apart. This is the journey of how we found the way.

Client
Curated British designer platform
Sector
Contemporary womenswear & jewellery
My role
Brand & digital discovery strategy
Focus
Editorial, search visibility, trend mapping

1Chapter one · Where it began

Between editorial and retail.

The client curates collections from a network of independent studios across the British Isles.

  • Sustainability-led production
  • Heritage textiles
  • Limited runs
  • Made to order

It is not a volume retailer. It sits somewhere between an editorial discovery space and a selective boutique, serving a considered customer who wants to know who made something, and why.

Until now, that story had mostly been told in person: in the room, with the garment in hand.

2Chapter two · The crossroads

From the room to the screen.

The brief: connect an audience spread across the country, and design a marketing structure that could grow with it, while the business moved from physical retail towards online discovery.

From

Physical presence

  • Reliance on pop-ups and in-person retail
  • Stories told in the room, not online
  • Discovery limited by geography
→
To

Digital discovery

  • An online destination for British design
  • Storytelling customers can find and read
  • A framework that works for years, not one campaign
3Chapter three · Finding the way

Built around the brand, not a template.

I did not reach for a standard marketing playbook. I started with the client's goals and identity, and shaped every recommendation to fit them.

The work moved through four stations.

  1. 1

    Listen

    I began with the goals, the leadership culture and the many designer identities living under one roof.

  2. 2

    Map

    I read seasonal runway and industry trend signals, and matched them to stories the brand was already telling: craft, texture, layering and material heritage.

  3. 3

    Structure

    I built a keyword and editorial framework covering blog themes, styling guidance and tone of voice.

  4. 4

    Hand over

    I left a repeatable system the team can keep using, so nobody starts from a blank page each season.

4Chapter four · The customer's path

From inspiration to purchase.

The framework gives a customer a clear path through the site, without turning a discovery platform into a hard-sell shop.

  1. Search

    Found through considered keywords

  2. Read

    Editorial and designer stories

  3. Style

    Guidance on how to wear it

  4. Buy

    A considered purchase

5Chapter five · The tension

Visibility without dilution.

Here was the real tension. Search visibility mattered a great deal, but chasing it too hard would flatten a sustainability-led, story-first brand. The strategy had to hold both.

Search visibilityBrand identity

Held at the centre: keywords that serve the storytelling, not replace it.

6Chapter six · What changed

Value created.

By the end of the journey, the work had strengthened the client on four fronts.

Strategic

A real move online

Supported the shift away from reliance on pop-ups towards a stronger digital discovery platform.

Brand

Positioning reinforced

Strengthened the client's place as a curated destination for contemporary British design.

Digital

Clearer navigation

The keyword and editorial structure created clear routes across styling guidance and blog pages.

Commercial

Browsing to buying

Moved visitors from inspiration-led browsing to structured engagement, with the identity intact.

7Chapter seven · The road ahead

Where I would take it next.

  1. iCompetitor keyword benchmarking, to see where the client can own search ground.
  2. iiClear performance measures showing how editorial content turns into engagement and sales over time.
  3. iiiA plan to reassure partner designers that moving online adds to their presence rather than taking from it.
